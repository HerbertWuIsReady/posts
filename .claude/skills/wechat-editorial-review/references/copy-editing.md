# Chinese copy-editing and voice preservation

Use this reference after structural, factual, and methodological review. Do not polish a sentence whose underlying claim is still wrong.

## Preserve before improving

Identify what already makes the article recognizable: directness, cadence, humor, technical density, colloquial terms, strong examples, or deliberate repetition. Make local repairs. A cleaner sentence is not better if it erases the author's voice or changes the claim.

## Reader-facing checks

- Each paragraph should make one main move and lead naturally to the next.
- Prefer concrete actors, actions, objects, and consequences over abstract noun stacks.
- Keep technical terms stable; do not vary terminology merely to avoid repetition.
- Explain a difficult concept near the example that needs it.
- Let some sentences be ordinary. Not every paragraph needs a slogan or final punchline.
- Cut repeated explanation only when the reader loses nothing needed for orientation.

## High-value flags

Flag clusters rather than mechanically banning individual devices:

- repeated “不是 X，而是 Y” constructions;
- “真正、唯一、永远、完全、必然、接近于零、免费”等绝对化措辞;
- every section ending in a quotable maxim;
- excessive em dashes used for unrelated functions;
- multiple metaphors competing in one passage;
- balanced triads and mirror-image paragraphs repeated until the outline becomes visible;
- unsupported superlatives disguised as confidence;
- generic AI polish such as “核心在于、归根结底、这改变了一切、这只是开始”.

Keep any instance that genuinely sharpens the argument. Explain the cost of the rest: false certainty, monotony, lost specificity, or a voice that sounds generated.

## Chinese typography

- Use full-width Chinese punctuation in Chinese prose.
- Add spaces between Chinese and English words, abbreviations, numerals with Latin units, and product names where house style requires it.
- Preserve official product capitalization.
- Use one form consistently for quotation marks, ranges, ellipses, and dashes.
- Keep straight quotes and syntax punctuation inside code blocks.
- Check repeated spaces, duplicated words, unmatched quotation marks, and inconsistent terminology.

## WeChat reading rhythm

- Test paragraphs on a narrow screen; visual line count matters more than source-line count.
- Break dense paragraphs at conceptual boundaries, not arbitrary character counts.
- Use tables only when comparison is the point.
- Explain what a chart proves; do not merely inventory its shapes.
- Keep headings informative enough for a reader who is scrolling.

## Line-edit finding format

For each material issue:

1. Quote only enough text to locate it.
2. Name the problem.
3. Explain the reader-facing consequence.
4. Offer the smallest revision that fixes it.

Do not fill the report with preference-level substitutions. Group repeated patterns and provide representative examples.
