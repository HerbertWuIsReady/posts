# Fact-checking and source discipline

Use this reference when an article contains checkable claims, quotations, dates, product behavior, metrics, customer outcomes, or competitive assertions.

## Classify before searching

| Claim type | Review question |
|---|---|
| Fact | Can a current, authoritative source confirm the exact statement? |
| Statistic | What is the source, date, population, denominator, method, and uncertainty? |
| Quote | Is the wording exact, attributed, dated, and presented in context? |
| Causal claim | Does the evidence identify a counterfactual, or only an association? |
| Product claim | Is this current documented behavior, roadmap, beta functionality, or marketing positioning? |
| Customer outcome | Is it independently measured, customer-stated, vendor-stated, estimated, or illustrative? |
| Inference | Are the premises sourced and the inferential step visibly labeled? |
| Example | Is it real, composite, hypothetical, or numerically illustrative? |

## Source hierarchy

Prefer the closest primary source that can answer the claim:

1. Official documentation, filings, standards, original research, datasets, direct transcripts, and customer statements.
2. Independent research and established reporting that exposes its evidence and method.
3. Reputable secondary explanations used for context, not as substitutes for available primary sources.

Vendor documentation is strong evidence for product vocabulary and documented behavior. It is weaker evidence for business impact, comparative superiority, and universal outcomes. Vendor case studies must be labeled as vendor-reported unless independently validated.

Search for disconfirming evidence as well as confirmation. Ten articles repeating one statistic may trace back to one unsupported source.

## Verdicts

- **Supported:** the source matches the wording, scope, date, and context.
- **Supported with qualification:** the substance is right but needs a condition, narrower scope, date, or attribution.
- **Mixed:** material parts are supported and unsupported.
- **Unsupported:** no adequate evidence found; absence of evidence is not proof of falsity.
- **Contradicted:** strong evidence conflicts with the claim.
- **Illustrative:** acceptable only when the article clearly labels it as constructed or hypothetical.

State confidence separately: high, medium, or low.

## Claim-ledger schema

Use one row per atomic claim:

| Location | Claim | Type | Verdict | Confidence | Best source | Required change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Split bundled claims. A sentence can contain a documented capability, an inferred mechanism, and an unsupported outcome.

## Citation rules

- Open every source before citing it.
- Cite the page that supports the claim, not a search-results page.
- Record publication or update date when time could change the answer.
- Keep quotations short and exact; paraphrase when the exact wording is unnecessary.
- Never convert a translation, summary, or model-generated explanation into an official quote.
- If a source does not disclose a metric, say so. Do not reconstruct a precise number from marketing language.

## Repair options

Use the smallest repair that preserves the article's point:

1. Add a source and date.
2. Attribute the claim: “Palantir states…” or “Airbus reported…”.
3. Narrow the scope or add a condition.
4. Relabel the number or scenario as illustrative.
5. Recast as the author's inference and expose the premises.
6. Remove the claim when it contributes heat but not evidence.

Core claims require evidence. Peripheral unsourced detail should usually be removed rather than triggering disproportionate research.
